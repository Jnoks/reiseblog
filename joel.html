Where the Ocean Meets Your Soul

The World’s Most Breathtaking Beaches

From powder-soft white sands to dramatic coastlines kissed by turquoise waves, these beaches are more than destinations – they’re feelings.

Date: 26.01.2026 Reading time: 6 min. Read: 164 Shared: 18

Joel Naujoks

Author

I’ve been traveling since 2017, chasing moments instead of plans. I’m fascinated by snakes, love sharks, would happily spend weeks deep in the jungle, and can never get enough papaya. As long as there’s a next destination, I know I’m exactly where I’m meant to be.

TOP 5 beach destinations for 2026

Some beaches are worth a detour, others are worth the entire trip. The five places below have one thing in common: the moment you step onto the sand, the journey there stops mattering. I picked them after years of comparing notes with other travellers, and I went back to three of them more than once – which is the only recommendation that really counts.

What makes a beach unforgettable is rarely the sand alone. It is the water, the light in the late afternoon, and how far you have to walk before the crowd disappears. These five earned their place for very different reasons:

  1. The Maldives for the water. Three shades of blue between the lagoon and the reef, and a drop-off close enough to swim to before breakfast.
  2. Anse Source d’Argent, Seychelles for the granite boulders. Shallow, calm and almost impossible to photograph badly, but busiest between ten and two.
  3. Eagle Beach, Aruba for the wind. Wide, white and rarely crowded, with the trade winds keeping the afternoons bearable.
  4. Nakupenda, Zanzibar for the sandbank. It appears at low tide and is gone a few hours later, so the timing matters more than the weather.
  5. El Nido, Philippines for the cliffs. The beaches are small, but the limestone rising straight out of the sea is worth the boat ride.

Pro tipps

  1. Check the tide before you go, not after you arrive. On sandbanks and reef beaches it decides whether you get a postcard or wet shoes. Useful link.
  2. Use reef-safe sunscreen. Several of these places have already closed sections of their reef to visitors, and the usual filters are part of the reason.

None of this needs an expensive trip. Two of the five are reachable on a normal budget if you travel outside the school holidays and stay a few streets back from the water. What costs money is convenience, not the beach itself.

If you only have time for one, take the Maldives. Not because it is the most beautiful – that is a matter of taste – but because it is the one place on this list where the water looked exactly the way it does in the photographs.
